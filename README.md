# Portfolio benchmarks

A running teardown of personal sites, kept while rebuilding [siddansh.vercel.app](https://siddansh.vercel.app).
The question behind every entry: **what makes a skeptical founder contact this person?**

Not a link dump. Each site was fetched and read, and every row says what to take and what to leave.
Sites are re-checked as the list grows; the date column is when the page last returned HTTP 200.

## How entries are judged

| Lens | What I look for |
|---|---|
| Above the fold | Who they are, what they sell, and whether the ask is on the first screen |
| Proof | Numbers, case depth, links that can be verified by a stranger |
| Ask | Calendar, email, or nothing at all — and how many times it repeats |
| Qualification | Price, scope, availability, guarantees: what filters the wrong buyer before the call |
| Return | Any reason to come back (writing, changelog, live data) |

## Does publishing a price help?

**What's evidenced:** a public price cuts inbound volume and raises inbound quality, with pipeline
roughly flat. HockeyStack Labs, across 80 B2B SaaS companies and 31M unique visitors: form submissions
2.8% with transparent pricing versus 4.6% without, but submission-to-pipeline 17.50% versus 10.31%;
bounce was *higher* on transparent pages (42% vs 35%) —
[hockeystack.com](https://www.hockeystack.com/lab-blog-posts/state-of-pricing-demo-case-study-pages).
One practitioner A/B test points the same way: leads −8.7%, lead-to-opportunity +21.4%, pipeline flat —
[adamgoyette](https://adamgoyette.substack.com/p/should-you-put-pricing-on-your-landing) (no sample size given).

**Stated preference, not behaviour:** 81% of B2B buyers want to find pricing themselves (N=2,185,
TrustRadius) — [trustradius](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/trustradius-new-b2b-report-the-age-of-the-self-serve-buyer-is-here-301566981.html).

**Folklore:** "publish prices and revenue goes up." Pipeline was flat in both real datasets. The
"contact for pricing means 38% higher bounce" claim is contradicted by HockeyStack's own bounce numbers.

**Weakness, stated plainly:** every source specific to *solo consultants* is anecdote with no numbers.
The honest argument for publishing a floor is that it buys back the operator's own time, not that it
grows revenue.

## What this changed on siddansh.vercel.app

| Date | Change | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-22 | The free-value ask moved into the hero | Chartbeat: 55% of pageviews get under 15s of attention |
| 2026-09-22 | CTA repeated in the sticky nav | Consultant sites repeat it in nav, hero and footer |
| 2026-09-22 | Strongest case file opens expanded | Case depth is the most-requested B2B content |
| 2026-09-22 | Five-question FAQ before contact | Josh Lamb's FAQ handles the same objections pre-call |
| 2026-09-22 | Scroll depth, sections seen and CTA clicks recorded | You can't optimise attention you don't measure |
| 2026-09-22 | Numbers moved above the diagram on phones | 57% of viewing time is above the fold |
| 2026-09-22 | Case files became linkable, with prev/next and a per-case ask | Retrospectives that end in an ask convert; the strongest assets on other sites end in nothing |
| 2026-09-22 | "Still open" added to two case files | Not one of the six retrospectives studied admits a limitation; it's the cheapest differentiator available |

## Sources for the conversion claims

- Nielsen Norman Group, [scrolling and attention](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/): 57% of viewing time is above the fold.
- Nielsen Norman Group, [commitment levels](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/commitment-levels/).
- Nielsen Norman Group, [about-us information](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/about-us-information-on-websites/): on-site testimonials are discounted by readers.
- Chartbeat via [Time](https://time.com/12933/what-you-think-you-know-about-the-web-is-wrong/): 55% of pageviews get under 15 seconds of attention.
- Portent, [site speed and conversion](https://portent.com/blog/analytics/research-site-speed-hurting-everyones-revenue.htm).

Widely-quoted figures such as "73% of B2B decision-makers prefer case studies" trace only to aggregator
blogs, not to a primary source, so they are deliberately not used here.
